Ten more reasoning displays

Ideas 11–20, chosen to share nothing with the first ten. The sample is the same turn as before (“teach me inference infra engineering”: two off-topic source searches, a stub-only vault search, a draft page with zero sources). The first page was about showing what the harness did. These go further: some let you act on the reasoning, some show only its effects, one isn't visual at all.

11

Ambient line

the reasoning lives in the input box and then disappears

The extreme minimum. Nothing is added to the transcript. While the tutor works, the placeholder of your empty input box says what it's doing right now, one short present-tense phrase at a time. When the answer arrives, the line is gone. Nothing to scroll past, nothing saved.

teach me inference infra engineering

…

looking for something a person wrote about LLM serving · 2nd try

earlier in the same turn: “checking what you already know” → “reading the vault” → “nothing solid yet”

Good forStudying with no clutter at all. The live phrase answers “is it stuck?” in the one place your eyes already are.
CostsLeaves no record: you can't look back at why the tutor did something. Needs a present-tense phrase for each tool, which is cheap.
12

Grounding gauge

one instrument per message

Each tutor message gets a single dial showing how firmly it's grounded, from “memory” up to “sourced”. Under it, three short lines say why the needle sits where it does. You get the answer to “can I trust this?” in one glance, and the tools themselves never appear.

teach me inference infra engineering

Inference infrastructure engineering has no substantive sourced page in the vault, so I’ll build a beginner-friendly first leg from general systems knowledge…

  • no vault page covers this (closest: a stub)
  • 2 source searches, 0 relevant
  • no web search on this model route
Good forA trust signal readable at a glance, without the per-sentence work that provenance ink needs. Low needles on many messages add up to an argument for giving the tutor better sources.
CostsA single number hides nuance, since some sentences may be well sourced and others not. The scoring rule has to be explainable, and it is: vault hit, source count, route.
13

Roads not taken

the options it considered and dropped

Most displays show what the tutor did. This one shows what it didn't do, and why. At each decision point the options it considered are listed, with the dropped ones struck through and a reason beside them. You see that “search the web” was never available, which is a fact about your setup rather than a choice the tutor made.

teach me inference infra engineering
Where do I get the material?
✗Teach from the vault page
only a stub exists
✗Cite a canonical source
searches returned DADA2 and ImageNet
✗Search the web
not available: this model route has no search configured
✓Teach from general knowledge, labelled unverified
How do I start?
✗Explain the whole serving stack
no record of what you know yet
✓Calibrate with one question on batching
Good forShowing that the weak spots come from missing capabilities, not from the model's choices. It tells you what to fix (turn on search) rather than what to blame.
CostsThe harness knows unavailable options for certain (the tool list), but options the model considered and dropped have to come from the model's own summary, which could be made up after the fact.
14

Checkpoint

the reasoning asks you before it commits

When the tutor reaches a consequential fork, like teaching unverified material or writing a sourceless page, it doesn't decide alone. The reasoning turns into a short question with buttons. You can give it a link, upload a document, or let it continue from memory. This is the only design where you can change the reasoning, not just read it.

teach me inference infra engineering

Before I teach this

I couldn’t find anything a person wrote about inference serving: two source searches came back off-topic, and web search isn’t set up for this model. I can teach it from general knowledge, but none of it will be checked.

Your answer is remembered per topic, so it asks once, not every turn.

Good forYour core complaint. Human-written material gets asked for at exactly the moment it matters, and it puts the choice between speed and trust in your hands.
CostsInterrupts the flow, so it has to be rare: once per topic and only for teaching or writing unsourced material. Needs a harness-level pause-and-resume, a bit like the aside endpoint.
15

Storyboard

the turn as four comic panels

The turn is summarized into up to four panels, each with a simple line drawing and a one-line caption: the question arrives, the search comes back empty, the tutor writes a draft, the tutor asks a calibration question. It's a quick way to recall later what happened in a turn, by picture.

teach me inference infra engineering
A new subject. No record of you on it.
Two searches, only junk (ImageNet?).
A draft page, dashed: nothing cited.
So: one question on batching first.
Good forRecalling a turn weeks later, and friendlier to visual learners. The panels could go into the lesson notes as a small visual summary.
CostsNeeds a set of reusable drawings (search, write, read, grade, empty result…). Panels must come from templates, never generated images, which keeps it cheap and honest.

Source forecast

a weather report for what you're about to read

Before the answer, a short forecast of how the turn's information is sourced: the chance it comes from memory, how much vault coverage there is, any off-topic papers, and whether the vault got bigger. It's playful, but it frames the text before you read it instead of after.

teach me inference infra engineering
memory80%heavy cover
vault20%one stub
papers0/2off-topic showers
vault growth+1draft page

Inference infrastructure engineering has no substantive sourced page in the vault, so I’ll build…

Good forMemorable, and it frames the reading before it starts. The “vault growth” tile is a running reminder of whether you're building anything.
CostsThe metaphor gets old. It needs a proportion of memory vs sources per turn, which only real attribution (provenance ink, page 1) can give honestly.
17

Vault diff

only the consequences, as a code-review diff

Ignore the process completely and show what changed. Each turn ends with a diff of your vault and learner record, like a commit: added pages, changed frontmatter, new links, evidence recorded. Warnings are flagged inline, such as sources: []. A turn that changed nothing says “no changes”, which is itself informative.

teach me inference infra engineering

+ title: Inference infrastructure engineering: a learning path foundation
+ status: draft
+ sources: [] ⚠ written from memory: nothing to check against
+ prereqs: [] deepens: []

Good forAnswers your other complaint, “why isn't the vault filling up”, on every turn. Honest by construction: it's computed from files, not from what the model says it did.
CostsSays nothing about why. Needs a before-and-after snapshot of the pages each turn touched; cheap, since writes go through one place (Engram).
18

Narration

reasoning you listen to

Not visual at all. Each turn has a small play button that reads a 20-second spoken summary of the reasoning, with the matching transcript line highlighted as it plays. Useful while your eyes are on the question, on a walk, or if reading the reasoning isn't comfortable. The same text also serves screen-reader users.

teach me inference infra engineering
0:07 / 0:19
I haven’t taught you this before, so I looked for something a person wrote on it. The two sources I found were about genetics and image datasets, so I set them aside. Nothing in your vault covers it either, so I’m teaching from general knowledge and saying so, and I started a draft page to improve later.
Good forAccessibility and hands-free study. The summary has to be written for a listener, which also makes it better written.
CostsNeeds text-to-speech (the browser's built-in speech works offline, keeping with the app's no-CDN rule) and a short summary generated per turn.
19

Your words, read back

the reasoning painted onto your own message

Instead of annotating the tutor's reply, annotate your message: highlight each word by how the harness read it and what it matched. Here it shows the failure exactly: “inference” pulled in a genetics paper titled “sample inference”, while “infra engineering” found nothing. You learn how to phrase requests, and the harness's misreadings become visible.

teach me inference infra engineering
teach meread as a request to start a new subject → learn mode
inferencematched “DADA2: sample inference…” (bioinformatics): a keyword match, wrong field
infrano vault match; abbreviation not expanded to “infrastructure”
engineeringset the page's domain: systems-engineering

Tip under the message: try “LLM serving systems” for better sources.

Good forDebugging why retrieval went wrong, which is the step before every other failure. It teaches you to ask better and shows exactly where the harness needs smarter query expansion.
CostsNeeds each query's terms traced back to results. The search tools would return which terms matched, a modest change to Engram and the source tools.

Annotated moves

chess notation for a tutoring turn

The turn is written as a compact line of moves, each a short code for one action, and graded with chess annotation marks: ! a good move, ? a weak one, ?? a blunder, !? an interesting gamble. A reviewer, whether a rule set or a second model, annotates the tutor's play. Expert shorthand that fits a whole turn in one line.

teach me inference infra engineering
1. St (state)   Src?   An   Rp stub   Sv   Src??   Wp!?   Wp?   Nx   Qc!
Src?? retried the same source search with a reworded query and got ImageNet; should have fallen back to asking for a link. Wp!? a page with no sources is a gamble, but it gives the concept a home. Wp? duplicate write. Qc! probing before teaching, exactly per rule 3.

Inference infrastructure engineering has no substantive sourced page in the vault, so I’ll build…

Good forEvaluating the harness itself: a turn's quality in one line, easy to compare across runs and models. Great in an eval dashboard.
CostsOpaque to anyone who doesn't know the codes, so it's for you as builder, not for studying. The marks need a judge; rules can catch duplicates and empty searches for free.

Combinations worth considering

  • 14 + 17: ask before teaching unsourced material, then show the vault diff after. Together they address both of your complaints: sources before, vault growth after.
  • 11 + 12: a live line while the turn runs and a gauge when it's done. The quietest option that still tells you whether to trust the answer.
  • 19 + 20 as developer tools: retrieval tracing and move annotation for tuning the harness, off by default in study mode.
